# UX/UI Team and AI Tools — Speech Draft

> Context: 2–3 minute spoken segment for the Advancing Responsible AI workshop at IIIT-Bangalore, 16 June 2026. Covers how the UX/UI team has integrated AI tools into their design workflow alongside Figma.

---

Let me tell you about something that has quietly changed how our design team works.

For years, Figma was the tool. Full stop. Our UX and UI team lived in Figma — wireframes, components, prototypes, handoffs. It was the standard, and it still is. But over the last year, something shifted.

Our designers started asking: what if we didn't have to start from a blank canvas every time?

Today, they use a combination of tools depending on what they're building. For complex application design — where UX flows matter, where you need to think through user journeys, accessibility, and design systems — they still use Figma. But now they bring Claude in alongside it. Claude helps them think through design decisions faster. It challenges assumptions, suggests alternatives, and helps articulate UX rationale that used to take hours of iteration to arrive at.

For quick turnarounds — a website, a microsite, a landing page — they use Lovable or Bolt.new. These tools let a designer go from a brief to a working, visually presentable prototype in a fraction of the time it used to take.

The result? Our team is saving roughly **[X]%** of the time they previously spent on early-stage design work. That time goes back into thinking more carefully, iterating more, and getting to higher quality outcomes faster.

We've already seen this in practice. **create.mosip.io** and the **Claim169** website were both built with this new workflow. What would have taken weeks of back-and-forth between designers and developers was turned around significantly faster — without compromising on quality or adherence to UX principles.

And I want to be clear about what this is not. This is not AI replacing designers. Our team still makes every design decision. They own the UX guidelines, the accessibility standards, the visual language. What AI does is remove the friction — the blank canvas problem, the first-draft paralysis, the time spent on scaffolding that has nothing to do with creativity.

What we are learning is that the highest value a designer brings is judgment — knowing what is right for the user. AI accelerates everything around that judgment. It does not replace it.

That, I think, is the honest and useful story here.
